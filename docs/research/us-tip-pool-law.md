# US tip-pool law constraints

Research for issue [#5](https://github.com/Opsell1/opsell/issues/5), child of the map [#1](https://github.com/Opsell1/opsell/issues/1).
Researched 2026-10-14.
This is product research, not legal advice; each Organization stays responsible for its own compliance and should have counsel review its tip policy.

## Question

What do US federal law (FLSA, including the 2018 tip amendments and the DOL tip regulations) and the major state variations require of a tip pool, such that Opsell's Tip Distribution is compliant?
Cover who may and may not share, tip credit interactions, record-keeping, and the states that differ most.

## Context

Opsell computes the Tip Distribution (tips collected during a shift, split among eligible Employees in proportion to hours worked).
It does not pay out tips; payment happens through payroll.
Roles in scope: GM, Manager, Senior-sup (shift lead: runs shifts, drafts Closing Reports, enters tips, no disciplinary powers), Employee.
Employees may work shifts at more than one Location, including Locations in different states.

## Federal baseline (FLSA, 29 CFR 531 and 516)

The 2018 amendment added FLSA section 3(m)(2)(B); DOL implemented it in the 2020 tip rule (85 FR 86756) as amended in 2021 (86 FR 52973).

### No one on the employer side may keep tips

The ban on employers keeping tips "applies regardless of whether an employer takes a tip credit" ([29 CFR 531.54(b)(1)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/part-531/section-531.54)).
Employers may not require employees "to share tips with managers or supervisors, as defined in § 531.52(b)(2), or employers" (same).
"An employer may not allow managers and supervisors to keep any portion of an employee's tips, regardless of whether the employer takes a tip credit" ([29 CFR 531.52(b)(2)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/part-531/section-531.52)).
A manager or supervisor "may keep tips that he or she receives directly from customers based on the service that he or she directly and solely provides" (same).

### Who counts as a "manager or supervisor"

The term means "any employee whose duties match those of an executive employee as described in § 541.100(a)(2) through (4) or § 541.101" ([29 CFR 531.52(b)(2)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/part-531/section-531.52)).
That is a duties test only; the salary test in 541.100(a)(1) is deliberately left out, so an hourly manager can still be excluded.
The three duties, all of which must be met ([29 CFR 541.100(a)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/part-541/section-541.100)):

- primary duty is management of the enterprise or of a customarily recognized department or subdivision;
- customarily and regularly directs the work of two or more other employees;
- has authority to hire or fire, or their recommendations on hiring, firing, advancement, promotion or other change of status are given particular weight.

Section 541.101 additionally covers anyone who owns at least a 20% equity interest and is actively engaged in management.

### Who may share depends on whether the employer takes a tip credit

With a tip credit, a mandatory pool may include only "employees who customarily and regularly receive tips" ([29 CFR 531.54(c)(1)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/part-531/section-531.54)).
In that case "the employer must notify its employees of any required tip pool contribution amount, may only take a tip credit for the amount of tips each employee ultimately receives" ([531.54(c)(2)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/part-531/section-531.54)).
Without a tip credit (full cash minimum wage), the pool "may include dishwashers, cooks, or other employees in the establishment who are not employed in an occupation in which employees customarily and regularly receive tips" ([531.54(d)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/part-531/section-531.54)).
In both cases the employer, managers and supervisors may not receive tips from the pool ([531.54(c)(3), (d)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/part-531/section-531.54)).
Federal law sets no maximum contribution percentage for a mandatory pool ([531.54(a)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/part-531/section-531.54)), so an hours-proportional split is permitted.

### Tip credit mechanics

The tip credit equals the minimum wage minus a cash wage of at least $2.13 per hour, and the employer must show the employee actually received at least that much in tips ([29 CFR 531.59](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/part-531/section-531.59)).
The credit is available only for hours in an occupation in which the employee is a tipped employee ([531.59(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/part-531/section-531.59)), and a dual-job employee is tipped only for the tipped occupation ([29 CFR 531.56(e)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/part-531/section-531.56)).
The 2021 "80/20" substantial-duties rule was removed from 531.56 in December 2024 (89 FR 101887, listed in the section history) after a court vacated it.
Before taking the credit, the employer must tell tipped employees the cash wage, the credit amount, that tips are retained except for a pool limited to customarily tipped employees, and that the credit does not apply without this notice ([531.59(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/part-531/section-531.59)).

### Timing

An employer that collects and redistributes tips must "fully distribute any tips the employer collects no later than the regular payday for the workweek in which the tips were collected", or as soon as practicable after it when amounts cannot be known before payroll ([531.54(b)(2)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/part-531/section-531.54)).

### Records

For each tipped employee whose wage uses the tip credit, the employer keeps ([29 CFR 516.28(a)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/part-516/section-516.28)):

- a notation identifying each employee whose wage is determined in part by tips;
- the weekly or monthly tips reported by the employee (IRS Form 4070 is acceptable);
- the tip credit amount per hour, reported to the employee in writing each time it changes;
- hours worked each workday in non-tipped occupations and the straight-time pay for them;
- hours worked each workday in tipped occupations and the straight-time earnings for them.

An employer that collects tips to run a mandatory pool without a tip credit keeps the notation and the reported tip amounts ([516.28(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/part-516/section-516.28)).
Payroll records are preserved for at least 3 years ([29 CFR 516.5](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/part-516/section-516.5)).

## State variations

States may be stricter than the FLSA, and the stricter rule wins.
Per DOL's state tipped-wage table (revised 2026-07-01), Alaska, California, Minnesota, Montana (larger businesses), Nevada, Oregon and Washington allow no tip credit; Michigan is phasing its tipped cash wage up to 50% of minimum wage by 2031 ([DOL WHD, Minimum Wages for Tipped Employees](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/state/minimum-wage/tipped)).

### California

No tip credit: "an employer cannot use an employee's tips as a credit towards its obligation to pay the minimum wage" ([DLSE Tips and Gratuities FAQ](https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/FAQ_tipsandgratuities.html)).
Labor Code 351 prohibits employers and their agents from sharing in or keeping any portion of a gratuity (same).
Mandatory pooling is allowed "so long as the tip pooling policy is not used to compensate the owner(s), manager(s) or supervisor(s) of the business, even if these individuals should provide direct table service" (same).
DLSE ties eligibility to employees whose service "bears a relationship to the customers' overall experience" (same), and its 1998 opinion letter took the narrower view that cooks and dishwashers cannot be included ([DLSE opinion 1998-12-28](https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/opinions/1998-12-28-1.pdf)); later appellate case law is commonly read as allowing back-of-house staff in the chain of service, so the safe product default is to let the Organization decide and record it.
A pool split that is patently unfair or unreasonable violates section 351 (same 1998 letter).
Card tips must be paid "not later than the next regular payday following the date the patron authorized the credit card payment" ([DLSE FAQ](https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/FAQ_tipsandgratuities.html)).
Shift supervisors: in [Chau v. Starbucks (Cal. Ct. App. 2009)](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2009/d053491m/), the court held section 351 does not bar shift supervisors who generally spend over 90% of their time on the same service work as baristas from sharing a collective tip box in proportion to hours worked, even if they are "agents" under section 350(d).
The court stressed that its holding was limited to those facts.
Gaps: the text of Labor Code 350, 351 and 353 (gratuity records) could not be fetched from leginfo.legislature.ca.gov in this session (bot protection), so the agent definition and the 353 record requirement were not read first-hand.

### New York

Eligibility is "based upon duties and not titles"; eligible employees "perform, or assist in performing, personal service to patrons at a level that is a principal and regular part of their duties and is not merely occasional or incidental" ([12 NYCRR 146-2.14(e)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/12-NYCRR-146-2.14)).
Listed eligible occupations: wait staff, counter personnel who serve food or beverages, bus persons, bartenders, service bartenders, barbacks, food runners, captains who provide direct food service, and hosts who greet and seat guests (same).
An employer may require food service workers to pool and "may set the percentage to be distributed to each occupation", and "only food service workers may receive distributions from the tip pool" ([146-2.16(b)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/12-NYCRR-146-2.16)).
A food service worker is primarily engaged in serving food or beverages and regularly receives tips; the classification is lost on any day the employee spends two hours or more, or more than 20% of the shift, in a non-tipped occupation ([146-3.4](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/12-NYCRR-146-3.4)).
So in New York, cooks and dishwashers are out of a mandatory pool even if the employer pays full minimum wage.
Employers may not require a greater share to indirectly tipped employees "than is customary and reasonable" ([146-2.14(f)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/12-NYCRR-146-2.14)).
Labor Law 196-d bars the employer, "his agent or an officer or agent of any corporation" from taking any part of employees' gratuities ([NY Labor Law 196-d](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/LAB/196-D)).
Shift supervisors: [Barenboim v. Starbucks (N.Y. 2013)](https://law.justia.com/cases/new-york/court-of-appeals/2013/122.html) drew the exclusion line at "meaningful or significant authority or control over subordinates", such as disciplining, assisting in performance evaluations or participating in hiring and firing; shift supervisors who mainly serve customers and lack that authority may share.
Records: employers running a pool must keep for at least six years a daily log of tips collected by each employee on each shift (cash or card), the list of eligible occupations, the share each occupation is scheduled to receive, and the amount each employee receives from the pool by date ([146-2.17](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/12-NYCRR-146-2.17)).

### Washington

"'Tip crediting' isn't allowed" ([WA L&I, Tips and Service Charges](https://www.lni.wa.gov/workers-rights/wages/tips-and-service-charges)).
An employer-established pool "can include employees who are not directly serving a customer, such as kitchen staff and hourly lead workers", and "cannot include salaried-exempt managers and business owners" (same).
"Employers, supervisors, or managers may accept tips only for services they directly provide" (same).
L&I's administrative policy ES.A.12 ties the exclusion to employees exempt from the Minimum Wage Act as executive, administrative or professional under RCW 49.46.010(3) ([ES.A.12](https://www.lni.wa.gov/workers-rights/_docs/esa12.pdf)); its text was summarized through search because the PDF could not be extracted locally.
Records: "Employers must record the amount paid each pay period to their employee, including tips", and L&I recommends clearly written tip-pool policies ([WA L&I](https://www.lni.wa.gov/workers-rights/wages/tips-and-service-charges)).

### Massachusetts

The Tips Act allows pools only among "wait staff employee", "service employee" and "service bartender"; no employer may require participation in a pool that distributes to anyone else ([M.G.L. c.149 s.152A(c)](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXXI/Chapter149/Section152A)).
A wait staff employee "has no managerial responsibility during a day in which the person serves beverages or prepared food or clears patrons' tables" (s.152A(a)).
"Employer" includes "any person whose primary responsibility is the management or supervision of wait staff employees" (same).
A service bartender prepares drinks served by other employees, so back-of-house cooks and dishwashers cannot share even with full minimum wage.
Tips remitted to the employer must be paid out "by the end of the same business day, and in no case later than the time set forth for timely payment of wages under section 148" (s.152A(e)).
Violations carry restitution with 12% interest and the civil and criminal penalties of section 27C (s.152A(f)).
Shift supervisors: the First Circuit held in [Matamoros v. Starbucks (2012)](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca1/12-1277/12-1277-2012-11-09.html) that shift supervisors are not wait staff because of their managerial responsibility and so may not share in the baristas' pool.

## Constraints on Opsell's product design

1. **Eligibility is per shift and per duty, not per Role.**
   The Tip Distribution must decide eligibility from what the person did on that shift (front-of-house service, back-of-house, managing), because federal tip-credit rules, NY's 2-hour/20% rule and MA's "during a day" rule all turn on duties that day.
2. **GM and Manager are excluded from every pool by default.**
   They match the federal executive duties test and are excluded in all four states; the product should not allow them a share, only optionally record tips they received directly and solely for their own service, outside the pool.
3. **Senior-sup may share federally and in CA, NY and WA, but not in MA.**
   Because Senior-sup has no hire, fire or discipline powers, it fails the federal 541.100(a)(4) test and the NY Barenboim line, matches Chau in CA, and fits WA's "hourly lead workers".
   In MA a shift lead with managerial responsibility that day is not wait staff (Matamoros), so the default for MA Locations must exclude Senior-sup from the pool on days they run the shift.
   If an Organization's toggles ever give Senior-sup input into discipline, scheduling or hiring, eligibility must be re-evaluated.
4. **Back-of-house inclusion is a per-Location policy gated by state and tip credit.**
   Allowed federally only when the Location takes no tip credit; prohibited in NY and MA even then; allowed in WA; contested in CA (left to the Organization, recorded).
5. **Pool rules are Location-scoped and state-aware.**
   An Employee covering a shift at another Location is pooled at the Location where the hours were worked, under that Location's state rules, and must not be pooled twice.
6. **Hours-proportional split is permitted but should allow occupation weights.**
   Federal law has no cap, NY lets the employer set per-occupation percentages and caps indirectly tipped shares at "customary and reasonable", and CA requires the split to be fair and reasonable.
7. **Keep an immutable, auditable record per shift.**
   Store tips collected per Employee per shift (cash and card), the eligible occupations and their scheduled shares, hours per Employee per shift split into tipped and non-tipped work, and the amount each Employee received from the pool by date.
   This covers 29 CFR 516.28 and NY 146-2.17; retain for at least 6 years (NY), not just the federal 3.
8. **Support payroll timing.**
   Distributions must be final in time for the regular payday (federal, CA card tips), or the same business day for tips remitted to the employer in MA, so Opsell's export to payroll must be ready by then.
9. **Tip credit is a per-Location setting validated against the state.**
   Disallow it for Locations in AK, CA, MN, MT, NV, OR and WA, and when enabled, record the written tip credit notice and per-hour credit amount changes.
10. **Show Employees their own records.**
    Employees can see their own tips, which supports the federal notice of required pool contributions and the transparency the states expect.

## Gaps and follow-ups

- California Labor Code 350, 351 and 353 text, and the current WA ES.A.12 text, should be read first-hand before implementation.
- Local rules (for example Chicago's tip credit phase-out and DC's Initiative 82 schedule) and other states (Oregon, Minnesota, Nevada, Illinois, New Jersey) were not researched.
- Service charges are treated differently from tips in several states (MA s.152A(d), WA, CA O'Grady) and are out of scope here.
