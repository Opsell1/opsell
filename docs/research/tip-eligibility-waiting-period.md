# Tip eligibility waiting periods

Research for issues [#50](https://github.com/Opsell1/opsell/issues/50) and [#54](https://github.com/Opsell1/opsell/issues/54) (duplicates), children of the map [#1](https://github.com/Opsell1/opsell/issues/1).
Researched 2026-10-18.
This is product research, not legal advice; each Organization stays responsible for its own compliance and should have counsel review its tip policy.

## Question

Is a tip eligibility waiting period lawful, where an Employee in a tip-eligible Position joins the mandatory Tip Pool only from their Nth day worked (the founder's former bakery used the 5th day)?
Cover federal law and CA, NY, MA, TX, plus the states where Opsell blocks tip credits (AK, MN, MT, NV, OR, WA).
For each: may a mandatory pool exclude an otherwise eligible tipped Employee during a waiting period, does it matter whether that Employee's own tips go into the pool, does a tip credit change the answer, and what notice or records are required.

## Context

Opsell computes the Tip Distribution and does not pay it out.
A Tip Pool is one Location's card tips plus counted cash jar for one Business Day; cash an Employee declares as received directly stays with that Employee.
The split is hours x Position weight among eligible Employees; eligibility is the eligible shift Position, minus fixed legal rules, plus an effective-dated Location Tip Policy.
Tip credit is a per-Location setting, blocked in AK, CA, MN, MT, NV, OR and WA.
The general federal and CA/NY/WA/MA pool rules are in [us-tip-pool-law.md](us-tip-pool-law.md) and are not repeated here.

## The two cases that matter

Every jurisdiction below gives a different answer depending on which of these a waiting period produces:

- **Case A - excluded, contributes nothing.**
  The new Employee shadows or trains, takes no payments of their own, and is simply not given a share of tips left for others.
- **Case B - excluded, but their own tips go in.**
  The new Employee serves customers, takes card payments or attracts jar tips, and those tips flow into the Location's Tip Pool while the Employee receives nothing back.

Because Opsell's Tip Pool takes every card tip recorded in the POS for the Location and Business Day, any trainee who closes a check lands in Case B by default.
Case B is the core legal problem everywhere; Case A is usually fine.

No court decision, agency opinion letter or Field Operations Handbook passage addressing trainees or probationary Employees in a tip pool was found in this session (see Gaps).
The verdicts below therefore apply general rules to the waiting-period facts.

## Federal (FLSA)

### Rules that apply

Tips belong to employees: "An employer may exert control over an employee's tips only to distribute tips to the employee who received them, require employees to share tips with other employees in compliance with § 531.54, or ... distribute tips to employees in a tip pool in compliance with § 531.54" ([29 CFR 531.52(b)(1)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/part-531/section-531.52)).
"An employer does not violate section 3(m)(2)(B)'s prohibition against keeping tips if it requires employees to share tips with other employees who are eligible to receive tips" ([29 CFR 531.54(b)(1)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/part-531/section-531.54)).
"Section 3(m)(2)(A) does not impose a maximum contribution percentage on mandatory tip pools" ([531.54(a)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/part-531/section-531.54)).
Nothing in 29 CFR 531.50-531.60 requires that every customarily tipped employee be included in a pool, or that every contributor receive a share.
With a tip credit, the employer "must notify its employees of any required tip pool contribution amount, may only take a tip credit for the amount of tips each employee ultimately receives, and may not retain any of the employees' tips for any other purpose" ([531.54(c)(2)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/part-531/section-531.54)).
"If the employee received less than the maximum tip credit amount in tips, the employer is required to pay the balance so that the employee receives at least the minimum wage" ([29 CFR 531.59(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/part-531/section-531.59)).
The tip credit "may be taken only for hours worked by the employee in an occupation in which the employee qualifies as a 'tipped employee'" (same), so classroom or non-serving training hours never carry a tip credit.
DOL's [Fact Sheet #15](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/15-tipped-employees-flsa) restates that an employer "may only take a tip credit for tips each tipped employee ultimately receives".

### Verdict

- Case A: **allowed**.
  The pool may simply not include the trainee.
- Case B: **allowed by the regulatory text, on conditions**.
  No federal rule bars a contribution of 100% with nothing back, and the tips stay with other eligible employees.
  With a tip credit, the credit for the trainee's hours is zero because they "ultimately receive" no tips, so the full cash minimum wage is owed for those hours, and the pool must still be limited to customarily tipped employees.
  The employer must notify the trainee of the required contribution amount (531.54(c)(2)).
  The pre-2020 Field Operations Handbook 30d04 rule that contributions be "customary and reasonable" (quoted by the [Texas Workforce Commission](https://efte.twc.texas.gov/tip_pooling.html)) was superseded by 531.54(a)'s "no maximum contribution percentage".
- Records: the general 29 CFR 516.28 duties apply (see the earlier doc); the trainee's zero pool share and the full-wage hours must be visible in the export.

## Texas

Texas has no separate tip-pool statute; TWC applies 29 CFR 531.54 and recommends that "It is a good practice to put the tip-sharing policy in writing and have everyone acknowledge it" ([TWC, Tip-Pooling / Tip-Sharing](https://efte.twc.texas.gov/tip_pooling.html)).
Verdict: same as federal.
Case A **allowed**; Case B **allowed only with full cash minimum wage for the waiting-period hours** and written notice of the contribution.

## California

### Rules that apply

Labor Code 351: "Every gratuity is hereby declared to be the sole property of the employee or employees to whom it was paid, given, or left for" ([Cal. Lab. Code 351](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/labor-code/lab-sect-351/), read via FindLaw because leginfo blocks automated reads).
DLSE: section 351 "has been interpreted to allow for involuntary tip pooling", but "the policy must be fair and reasonable", and the employer may require sharing "so long as the employees that share in the tip pooling policy are employees to whom the tip was paid, given, or left for" ([DLSE Tips and Gratuities FAQ, Q4](https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/FAQ_tipsandgratuities.html)).
The DLSE 1998 opinion letter, reading *Leighton v. Old Heidelberg, Ltd.* (1990) 219 Cal.App.3d 1062, says the statute's purpose "was to ensure that employees, not employers, receive the full benefit of gratuities that patrons intend for the sole benefit of those employees who serve them" and requires "some reasonable relationship between the degree to which the employee or category of employee provides such table service and the distribution of pooled tips"; "a tip pooling distribution that is patently unfair or unreasonable would violate Labor Code §351" ([DLSE Opinion 1998.12.28-1](https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/opinions/1998-12-28-1.pdf)).
Labor Code 353: "Every employer shall keep accurate records of all gratuities received by him, whether received directly from the employee or indirectly by means of deductions from the wages of the employee or otherwise" ([Cal. Lab. Code 353](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/labor-code/lab-sect-353/)).
No tip credit exists in CA, so the tip-credit question does not arise.

### Verdict

- Case A, trainee only shadowing and not serving: **allowed**; tips were not "left for" someone who did not serve.
- Case A, trainee serving as part of the team but taking no payments: **unsettled, leaning against**.
  A Location-wide daily pool treats the tips as left for everyone in the chain of service; giving a zero share to someone who served has no "reasonable relationship" to their service.
- Case B: **likely unlawful**.
  Diverting tips paid to the trainee to others while the trainee gets nothing contradicts "sole property of the employee or employees to whom it was paid", and is the kind of "patently unfair" distribution the DLSE letter calls a 351 violation.
- Note: *Lu v. Hawaiian Gardens Casino* (2010) holds section 351 has no private right of action, but the Labor Commissioner can cite and recover under 351(b), and other claims (conversion, PAGA) remain; this lowers lawsuit risk, not the rule.

## New York

### Rules that apply

Labor Law 196-d: no employer, agent "or any other person shall demand or accept, directly or indirectly, any part of the gratuities, received by an employee", with a carve-out for "the sharing of tips by a waiter with a busboy or similar employee" ([NY Labor Law 196-d](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/LAB/196-D)).
"An employer may require food service workers to participate in a tip pool and may set the percentage to be distributed to each occupation from the tip pool. Only food service workers may receive distributions from the tip pool" ([12 NYCRR 146-2.16(b)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/12-NYCRR-146-2.16)).
The NY DOL FAQ repeats that in a required pool "the employer may set the percentages of tips shared among food service workers" ([NY DOL Tips and Gratuities FAQ](https://dol.ny.gov/tips-and-gratuities-faq)).
Eligibility is "based upon duties and not titles" ([146-2.14(e)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/12-NYCRR-146-2.14)).
Tip credit: "An employer may take a credit towards the basic minimum hourly rate if a service employee or food service worker receives enough tips and if the employee has been notified of the tip credit as required in section 146-2.2" ([146-1.3](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/12-NYCRR-146-1.3)).
Notice: before employment, written notice of the regular rate, overtime rate, tip credit and payday, with a signed acknowledgment kept six years ([146-2.2](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/12-NYCRR-146-2.2)).
Records: a daily log of tips collected by each employee each shift, the eligible occupations, "the shares of tips that each occupation is scheduled to receive", and "the amount in tips that each employee receives from the tip share or tip pool, by date", kept six years and "regularly made available for participants" ([146-2.17](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/12-NYCRR-146-2.17)).
"No agreement between the employer and employee(s) can change or waive legal protections about the handling and payment of tips" ([NY DOL FAQ](https://dol.ny.gov/tips-and-gratuities-faq)).

### Verdict

- Case A: **allowed**, leaning; nothing requires a non-contributing worker to receive from a pool.
- Case B: **unsettled, leaning unlawful**.
  The regulation lets the employer set shares "to each occupation", not zero out an individual inside an eligible occupation; a trainee server is a food service worker by duties, and taking their tips into a pool they cannot draw from looks like "any other person" accepting their gratuities outside the 146-2.16 safe harbor.
  If a tip credit is taken, the trainee did not "receive enough tips", so no credit applies and full minimum wage is owed.
- A trainee who spends 2 hours or more, or over 20% of the shift, in non-tipped work (for example classroom training) is not a food service worker that day ([146-3.4](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/12-NYCRR-146-3.4)), so they may not receive from a mandatory pool that day and no tip credit applies.

## Massachusetts

### Rules that apply

"No employer or person shall cause, require or permit any wait staff employee, service employee, or service bartender to participate in a tip pool through which such employee remits any wage, tip or service charge, or any portion thereof, for distribution to any person who is not a wait staff employee, service employee, or service bartender" ([M.G.L. c.149 s.152A(c)](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXXI/Chapter149/Section152A), read via the [2025-12-10 Wayback snapshot](https://web.archive.org/web/20251210114905/https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXXI/Chapter149/Section152A) because malegislature.gov was under maintenance).
"No employer or other person shall demand, request or accept from any wait staff employee, service employee, or service bartender any payment or deduction from a tip or service charge given to such wait staff employee" (s.152A(b)).
Where the bill imposes a service charge or tip, "the total proceeds ... shall be remitted only to the wait staff employees, service employees, or service bartenders in proportion to the service provided by those employees" (s.152A(d)).
Restitution with 12% interest plus section 27C penalties (s.152A(f)).
Tip credit: the service rate applies only if the employee "has been informed by the employer of the provisions of this paragraph, and all tips received by such employee have been retained by the employee, except that this paragraph shall not be construed to prohibit the pooling of tips among employees who customarily and regularly receive tips"; the top-up is calculated "at the completion of each shift" and "may not exceed the value of the tips actually received by an employee" ([M.G.L. c.151 s.7](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXXI/Chapter151/Section7), read via Wayback).
The AG's advisory names the three classes who may share and does not address training ([AG Advisory 2004/3, rev. 2025](https://www.mass.gov/doc/attorney-generals-advisory-on-tips/download), read via a secondary summary).

### Verdict

- Case A: **allowed**.
- Case B: **unsettled, high risk**.
  Section 152A(c) only limits who receives, so a pool among wait staff that excludes a contributing trainee is not expressly barred; but (d) requires distribution "in proportion to the service provided" for tips on a bill, and Massachusetts courts read the Tips Act strictly (see Matamoros in the earlier doc).
  With the service-rate tip credit, the per-shift top-up is capped by tips "actually received", so the trainee must get full minimum wage for those shifts.

## Minnesota

### Rules that apply

"Any gratuity received by an employee or deposited in or about a place of business for personal services rendered by an employee is the sole property of the employee. No employer may require an employee to contribute or share a gratuity received by the employee with the employer or other employees or to contribute any or all of the gratuity to a fund or pool operated for the benefit of the employer or employees" ([Minn. Stat. 177.24 subd. 3](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/177.24)).
Voluntary sharing is allowed, but "The agreement to share gratuities must be made by the employees without employer coercion or participation", except the employer may, "upon the request of employees, safeguard gratuities to be shared by employees and disburse shared gratuities to employees participating in the agreement", report amounts for tax, and post the section (same).
Card tips: "the full amount of gratuity indicated in the payment must be distributed to the employee no later than the next scheduled pay period" (subd. 3a).
Rule: gratuities from customers served by more than one direct service employee "divided among the direct service employees is not a violation" ([Minn. R. 5200.0080 subp. 8](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/rules/5200.0080/)).
DLI: "Employers are prohibited from directing employees to pool or share tips", but "Dividing money left in a tip jar, or the equivalent of a tip jar, among direct service employees working on the same shift is not a violation", where a shift is "a period of time in which a particular group of employees work together to provide direct service to customers" ([DLI, Tip credit, tip sharing, tip pooling](https://dli.mn.gov/sites/default/files/pdf/tips.pdf)).
No tip credit.

### Verdict

- A mandatory Location-wide Tip Pool of card tips is itself **forbidden** in Minnesota, waiting period or not.
- Case B: **clearly forbidden**; the trainee's tips are their sole property.
- Case A for the tip jar: **likely forbidden** if the trainee is a direct service employee on that shift, since the DLI exception covers dividing the jar among the direct service employees on the shift, and an employer-set exclusion is employer "participation" in the terms.
- In a voluntary, employee-made agreement the employees, not the employer, could choose a waiting period; Opsell may compute it only as the employees' recorded agreement.

## Washington

### Rules that apply

An employer must pay "All tips and gratuities" to its employees, and tips "may not count towards, the employee's hourly minimum wage" ([RCW 49.46.020(3)](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=49.46.020)).
RCW 49.46.160 covers automatic service charge disclosure, not tip pools ([RCW 49.46.160](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=49.46.160)).
"Tips and gratuities due to an employee may not be used by the employer for any purpose except to administer a valid tip pool amongst qualifying employees"; "State law does not prohibit a tip pool amongst employees established by an employer"; "The department recommends that written policies be established to govern mandatory tip pooling arrangements, and that employees be informed of these policies" ([L&I ES.A.12, 2019-03-06](https://www.lni.wa.gov/workers-rights/_docs/esa12.pdf)).
"An employer may not require an employee to contribute more than the employee actually received in tips to a mandatory tip pool" (same, section D).
Where standards differ, "the standard more favorable or more protective to the employee is applied" (same, A.8).
[L&I's page](https://www.lni.wa.gov/workers-rights/wages/tips-and-service-charges) repeats the cap and the written-policy recommendation.

### Verdict

- Case A: **allowed**.
- Case B: **not expressly forbidden** (100% is not "more than" received), but **unsettled**: the trainee is a "qualifying employee" whose tips are "due to" them, and L&I applies the more protective standard.

## Oregon

Tips may not count toward the minimum wage ([ORS 653.035(3)](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors653.html)).
BOLI: "Your tips are yours and cannot be counted against your hourly pay. Your employer can require you to pool tips with other workers, but under federal regulations, management or the house cannot share in the pool" ([BOLI Minimum Wage FAQ](https://www.oregon.gov/boli/workers/pages/minimum-wage.aspx)).
No Oregon statute or rule on tip pool membership, contribution caps or notice was found; ORS chapter 652 contains no tip-pool text.
Verdict: Case A **allowed**; Case B **unsettled**, governed in practice by the federal rule (allowed, full minimum wage already required).

## Nevada

"It is unlawful for any person to: (a) Take all or part of any tips or gratuities bestowed upon the employees of that person. (b) Apply as a credit toward the payment of the statutory minimum hourly wage ... any tips" ([NRS 608.160](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/nrs-608.html)).
The Labor Commissioner: "Nevada case law permits mandatory tip pooling by employers. Employers may include employees in different positions and ranks in mandatory tip pools. However, the employer may not keep any portion of the tips for itself", citing *Wynn Las Vegas, L.L.C. v. Baldonado*, 129 Nev. 734 (2013) ([Advisory Opinion AO-2025-08](https://labor.nv.gov/uploadedFiles/labornvgov/content/Employer/AO-2025-08%20Tip%20Pooling%20Policy.pdf)).
No notice rule found.
Verdict: Case A **allowed**; Case B **likely allowed** under state law (the employer keeps nothing), subject to federal rules.

## Alaska

"An employer may handle an employee's tips only for the purpose of ... (2) redistributing tips to service employees under a tip pooling arrangement; an employer shall provide written notice to all service employees of the tip pooling arrangement; any change to a tip pooling arrangement must be provided in writing on or before the payday before the payday on which the change becomes effective; a tip pooling arrangement may not be retroactive" ([8 AAC 15.907(b)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/alaska/8-AAC-15.907), as amended 2021-10-27).
"Service employee" includes host, server, busser, dishwasher or cook and excludes an employee "whose primary duty is managerial or administrative"; a "tip pooling arrangement" is "an agreement under which a portion of an employee's tips is collected for distribution among service employees" (8 AAC 15.907(d)).
The 2021 amendment was noticed as "employer option for tip pooling", restoring employer pools that include back of house after the 2018 version had barred them ([Alaska DOLWD public notice, 2021](https://labor.alaska.gov/commish/proposed_regulations/8_AAC_15-907_Public_Notice_.pdf)).
No tip credit.
Verdict: Case A **allowed** if in the written notice; Case B **unsettled** (the arrangement is defined as collecting "a portion" of tips for distribution among service employees, and the trainee is one).
The notice rules bite directly on Opsell: the waiting period must be in the written notice, a change takes effect only from a payday after written notice, and never retroactively.

## Montana

"An employer may impose a tip pooling arrangement as provided in this section"; the pool may include "servers, hosts, bussers, dishwashers, cooks, or other employees"; there is no "minimum or maximum contribution percentage on mandatory tip pools; however, employees may not be required to contribute to a tip pool more than the amount received in tips"; the employer "shall notify its employees of any required tip pooling arrangement" and, when it collects tips for a mandatory pool, "shall maintain and preserve payroll or other records evidencing tips received and distributed" ([MCA 39-3-218](https://archive.legmt.gov/bills/MCA/title_0390/chapter_0030/part_0020/section_0180/0390-0030-0020-0180.html), 2025 MCA).
No tip credit.
Verdict: Case A **allowed**; Case B **not expressly forbidden** (mirrors federal text), **unsettled**; notice of the waiting period is required as part of the "required tip pooling arrangement".

## Constraints on Opsell's product design

1. **A waiting period may only remove an Employee as a recipient; it must never pool that Employee's own tips to others.**
   During the waiting period, tips attributable to the Employee (card tips on checks they closed, and cash they declared) must be routed back to that Employee in full, outside the Tip Pool, and their hours must be excluded from the split.
   This makes every waiting period a Case A, which is allowed or near-allowed everywhere except Minnesota.
   Opsell needs per-check attribution of card tips to the closing Employee to do this; if a Location cannot attribute, the waiting period must be unavailable at that Location.
2. **Never offer "pool their tips, give them nothing" (Case B), in any state.**
   It is only clearly safe federally and in Texas and Nevada, and even there it wipes out the tip credit for those hours; it is likely unlawful in CA and risky in NY, MA, WA, AK and MT.
   The product-quality cost of a per-state toggle is not worth the exposure.
3. **Disable the waiting period entirely in Minnesota.**
   Minnesota forbids employer-mandated pools; Opsell may only compute a voluntary employee agreement there, whose terms (including any waiting period) are the employees' own record, not a Tip Policy field.
4. **California default: off, with a warning.**
   Allow it only for Positions or shifts where the new Employee is shadowing and not serving; if they serve customers, a zero share risks the "reasonable relationship" test.
5. **New York: waiting period applies by duties, and the zero share must be explainable.**
   Because NY sets shares "to each occupation", prefer modelling the trainee as a distinct Position (trainee) with its own scheduled weight, which may be zero only while they take no tips, and record it in the 146-2.17 occupation list and daily log.
6. **Tip credit interaction.**
   For any Location with a tip credit (federal, TX, NY, MA), hours in a waiting period where the Employee keeps no tips must be flagged in the payroll export as hours owed the full cash minimum wage, and classroom or non-serving training hours are never tipped hours.
7. **Notice is part of the setting.**
   The waiting period must be in the Location Tip Policy text shown to Employees, with the effective date.
   Changes must be effective-dated forward only, never retroactive, and in Alaska no earlier than the payday after the payday on or before which written notice was given.
   Keep the acknowledgment of the notice (NY six years).
8. **Count days per Employment Period, on worked Business Days.**
   "Nth day worked" should count Business Days with a shift in an eligible Position, within the current Employment Period, across the Organization's Locations, so a rehire or a transfer behaves predictably; the counting rule must be stated in the notice.
9. **Defaults.**
   Default waiting period is 0 (none) everywhere.
   Where offered, it should be a Location Tip Policy field with a small maximum (the founder's bakery used 5 days) and an explicit acknowledgment that the Employee's own tips stay with them.
10. **Audit trail.**
    For each Business Day, record which Employees were excluded by the waiting period, the tips routed back to each of them, and the policy version in force, so the Distribution can be defended under 516.28, 146-2.17, Lab. Code 353, MCA 39-3-218 and 8 AAC 15.907.

## Gaps and follow-ups

- No case, opinion letter or agency guidance specifically on trainees, probationary periods or tips paid to a trainer was found; the verdicts above are applications of general rules, and Case B in CA, NY, MA, WA, AK and MT should go to counsel before launch.
- *Leighton v. Old Heidelberg* was read only through the DLSE 1998 letter's quotations (Justia and leginfo blocked automated reads); Labor Code 351 and 353 were read through FindLaw, not leginfo.
- *Lu v. Hawaiian Gardens Casino* (2010) 50 Cal.4th 592 was known only through secondary summaries.
- M.G.L. c.149 s.152A and c.151 s.7 were read from a 2025-12-10 Wayback snapshot because malegislature.gov was down; the MA AG advisory was read only through a secondary summary (mass.gov returned 403).
- 8 AAC 15.907 was read from Cornell LII, which may lag the official Alaska Administrative Code; the full 2021 adoption order was not read.
- The current DOL Field Operations Handbook chapter 30 was not read first-hand; the 30d04 text was read as quoted by TWC and is older than the 2020 rule.
- Oregon has no located rule on pool membership or notice; an Oregon BOLI opinion or administrative rule may exist that was not found.
- Local ordinances (for example Seattle, Chicago, DC) were not checked.
