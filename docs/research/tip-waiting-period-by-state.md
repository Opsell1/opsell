# Tip eligibility waiting period by state

Research for issue [#54](https://github.com/Opsell1/opsell/issues/54), building on [US tip-pool law constraints](us-tip-pool-law.md).
Researched 2026-10-18.
This is product research, not legal advice; each Organization stays responsible for its own compliance and should have counsel review its tip policy.

## Question

Can a Location keep a new Employee in a tip-eligible Position out of the Tip Pool until their 5th day worked?
For federal law and for CA, NY, MA, TX, AK, MN, MT, NV, OR and WA, this note answers:

1. May a mandatory pool exclude an otherwise eligible tipped Employee during a waiting period, or is that the employer "keeping" or diverting tips?
2. Does it matter whether the excluded Employee received tips directly, and where must those tips go?
3. Does a tip credit change the answer?
4. Are there notice, written policy or recordkeeping duties?
5. Where it is allowed, under what conditions; where it is not, what must Opsell do instead?

## Context

Opsell builds one Tip Pool per Location per Business Day from card tips plus the counted cash jar.
Cash tips an Employee declares directly stay with that Employee and never enter the pool.
The pool is split by hours times Position weight among eligible Employees.
Eligibility is the eligible shift Position, minus fixed legal rules, plus an effective-dated per-Location Tip Policy.
The founder's former bakery kept new Employees in tip-eligible Positions out of the pool until their 5th day worked; that is the setting under study.
The earlier research already fixed: GM and Manager never share; Senior-sup may share except in MA; BOH may share only without a tip credit and never in NY or MA; tip credit is blocked in AK, CA, MN, MT, NV, OR and WA; distributions are final by the regular payday; per-shift tip records are kept 6 years.

## Verdicts at a glance

| Jurisdiction | Verdict | Key reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Federal (FLSA) | Allowed with conditions | Employer may require sharing with other eligible employees; nothing requires every eligible employee to receive; tip credit falls to zero for tips the new hire does not keep |
| Texas | Allowed with conditions | State law adopts FLSA 3(m) and does not apply to FLSA-covered employers; federal rules govern |
| New York | Allowed with conditions | Barenboim: Labor Law 196-d gives no "affirmative right to participate" in a pool, with a possible "outer limit"; written notice and 6-year pool records |
| California | Unsettled, high risk | Labor Code 351 makes each gratuity the "sole property" of the employees it was left for, and Leighton reads a pool as equitable distribution among the employees who served; no authority on full exclusion of a server |
| Massachusetts | Unsettled, high risk | Tips Act bars the employer from demanding any part of a wait staff tip and allows only a "valid tip pool", undefined; strict liability and restitution with 12% interest |
| Alaska | Allowed with conditions | 8 AAC 15.907 lets the employer redistribute tips to service employees under a written pooling arrangement; changes in writing in advance and never retroactive |
| Minnesota | Clearly forbidden | Employer may not require any tip sharing or pool; pooling "may not be a condition of employment" |
| Montana | Allowed with conditions | MCA 39-3-218 lets the employer impose a pool with notice; no contribution beyond tips received; records |

| Washington | Allowed with conditions | L&I ES.A.12: mandatory pools allowed and state law does not specify who must be included, apart from exempt managers; written policy recommended |

No court decision or agency opinion squarely on a "new employee", "training period" or probationary exclusion from a tip pool was found in any jurisdiction.
Every verdict above is reasoned from the general pool rules.

## Federal baseline (FLSA)

### 1. Exclusion is not "keeping" if the tips go only to eligible employees

FLSA 3(m)(2)(B) says an employer "may not keep tips received by its employees for any purposes, including allowing managers or supervisors to keep any portion of employees' tips, regardless of whether or not the employer takes a tip credit" ([29 U.S.C. 203(m)(2)(B)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/29/203)).
The regulation lists what an employer may do with tips: pass them to the employee who received them, "require employees to share tips with other employees" under 531.54, or redistribute them in a pool it administers ([29 CFR 531.52(b)(1)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/29/531.52)).
The Field Operations Handbook restates this and says "Any other employer control over employees' tips constitutes keeping tips" ([FOH 30d02(b)](https://www.dol.gov/sites/dolgov/files/WHD/legacy/files/FOH_Ch30.pdf)).
Its examples of keeping are paying business expenses, sending tips to non-employees, and requiring sharing "with another employee who is not eligible to receive them" ([FOH 30d02(c)](https://www.dol.gov/sites/dolgov/files/WHD/legacy/files/FOH_Ch30.pdf)).
None of the rules says that every eligible employee, or every contributor, must receive a share.
The handbook expressly lets an employer "require a manager or supervisor to contribute tips to a mandatory tip pool" while barring them from receiving from it ([FOH 30d02(d), 30d07(d)](https://www.dol.gov/sites/dolgov/files/WHD/legacy/files/FOH_Ch30.pdf)).
So contributing without receiving is not, by itself, keeping.
"The FLSA does not restrict the amount of tips an employee can be required to contribute to eligible employees in a mandatory tip pool" ([FOH 30d07(c)](https://www.dol.gov/sites/dolgov/files/WHD/legacy/files/FOH_Ch30.pdf); also [29 CFR 531.54(a)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/29/531.54)).
Conclusion: under federal law a waiting period that withholds pool distributions from a new hire, while the pooled money goes only to other eligible Employees, is not keeping tips.

### 2. Direct tips

Federal law lets the employer require the new hire to put tips they received directly into the pool, since "Sharing with other tip-eligible employees" does not violate the keeping rule ([29 CFR 531.54(b)(1)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/29/531.54)).
There is no federal requirement that the new hire keep their own direct tips during the waiting period.
Opsell's existing rule that declared direct cash tips stay with the Employee is stricter than federal law requires and is fine.

### 3. Tip credit

With a tip credit, the employer "may only take a tip credit for the amount of tips each employee ultimately receives" ([29 CFR 531.54(c)(2)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/29/531.54)).
The handbook adds that pool contributions "must not lower the rate of the employee below the minimum wage", and that only tips "retained by the employee (either those not contributed to a tip pool or those received from the tip pool)" count ([FOH 30d08(a)-(b)](https://www.dol.gov/sites/dolgov/files/WHD/legacy/files/FOH_Ch30.pdf)).
The statute also requires that the employee was informed of 3(m) and that "all tips received by such employee have been retained by the employee", except for pooling "among employees who customarily and regularly receive tips" ([29 U.S.C. 203(m)(2)(A)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/29/203)).
A new hire who contributes their tips and draws nothing has retained no tips, so the available credit for them is zero for that period.
In practice the employer must pay that Employee the full cash minimum wage during the waiting period.
If the employer takes a credit anyway, the remedy is the credit taken plus liquidated damages, and the full minimum wage is owed ([FOH 30d05(b), 30d10](https://www.dol.gov/sites/dolgov/files/WHD/legacy/files/FOH_Ch30.pdf)).

### 4. Notice and records

A tip-credit employer "must notify its employees of any required tip pool contribution amount" ([29 CFR 531.54(c)(2)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/29/531.54)).
The tip credit notice must be given in advance and covers the cash wage, the credit amount and the tip-retention rule ([29 CFR 531.59(b)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/29/531.59)).
Collected tips must be fully distributed no later than the regular payday ([29 CFR 531.54(b)(2)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/29/531.54); [FOH 30d12](https://www.dol.gov/sites/dolgov/files/WHD/legacy/files/FOH_Ch30.pdf)).
Record duties under 29 CFR 516.28 are covered in the earlier note.

### 5. Trainees

Federal law has no trainee category for tips.
A new hire in a tip-eligible Position is an employee whose eligibility turns on their occupation, not tenure ([FOH 30d08(c)](https://www.dol.gov/sites/dolgov/files/WHD/legacy/files/FOH_Ch30.pdf)).
The tip credit is available only for hours in an occupation in which the employee is a tipped employee ([29 CFR 531.59(b)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/29/531.59)), so pure training time spent not serving customers cannot carry a tip credit.
Searches found no court decision on excluding trainees or new hires from a tip pool.

## Texas

The Texas Minimum Wage Act defines a tipped employee's wage as "the amount described as paid to a tipped employee under Section 3(m)" of the FLSA ([Tex. Lab. Code 62.052](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/LA/htm/LA.62.htm)).
The chapter does "not apply to a person covered by the Fair Labor Standards Act" ([Tex. Lab. Code 62.151](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/LA/htm/LA.62.htm)).
Texas has no separate tip-pool statute.
The Texas Workforce Commission's tip pooling page recommends that it "is a good practice to put the tip-sharing policy in writing and have everyone acknowledge it" ([TWC, Tip-Pooling / Tip-Sharing](https://efte.twc.texas.gov/tip_pooling.html)).
That page still repeats pre-2018 positions, such as a 15 percent contribution safe harbor and "The FLSA does not restrict tip pooling when no tip credit is taken", which the 2018 amendment and 29 CFR 531.54(a) superseded, so it is not relied on for the rules.
Verdict: same as federal, allowed with conditions.

## New York

### 1. Exclusion

Labor Law 196-d bars any employer, agent or "any other person" from demanding or accepting any part of an employee's gratuities, and permits "the sharing of tips by a waiter with a busboy or similar employee" ([NY Labor Law 196-d](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/LAB/196-D)).
"An employer may require food service workers to participate in a tip pool" and "may set the percentage to be distributed to each occupation from the tip pool" ([12 NYCRR 146-2.16(b)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/12-NYCRR-146-2.16)).
The exact question was certified to the New York Court of Appeals: "Does New York Labor law permit an employer to exclude an otherwise eligible tip-earning employee under § 196-d from receiving distributions from an employer-mandated" tip pool ([Barenboim v. Starbucks, 2d Cir. 2012, certification order](https://ww3.ca2.uscourts.gov/decisions/OPN/10-4912.11-3199_opn.pdf)).
The Second Circuit noted the district court had held that "who participates in a tip pool is a decision left to the employer's discretion", and laid out the employees' counter-argument that collecting tips from one group and paying them to another is itself retention (same).
The Court of Appeals' decision states that 196-d does "not grant an otherwise eligible employee an affirmative right to participate in tip pools or receive tip" distributions, and leaves "open the possibility that there may be an outer limit to an employer's ability to excise certain classifications" ([Barenboim v. Starbucks, 21 N.Y.3d 460 (2013)](https://www.nycourts.gov/reporter/3dseries/2013/2013_04754.htm)).
Both quotes come from CourtListener search snippets of the decision text, which do not show whether each line is from the majority or the separate opinion.
A law firm summary reports the court's examples of that limit as giving everything to a single employee or only to the highest-ranking employees, and that excluding assistant store managers was within the limit ([Proskauer summary](https://www.proskauer.com/alert/new-york-court-of-appeals-clarifies-important-questions-about-tip-sharing-statute)).
The excluded assistant managers in Barenboim also put their own tips in the shared jar, so the holding covers exclusion of contributors.
A short waiting period for every new hire is far from the "outer limit" examples, so it is likely allowed.

### 2. Direct tips

Barenboim allowed exclusion of employees whose tips went into the common jar, so the new hire's tips may be pooled.
A separate rule says a charge purported to be a gratuity "must be distributed in full as gratuities to the service employees or food service workers who provided the service" ([12 NYCRR 146-2.18(a)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/12-NYCRR-146-2.18)).
That rule is aimed at employer-imposed charges, but the Barenboim plaintiffs argued it also means the server who provided the service must receive a share; the Second Circuit said no New York court had construed it.
The conservative reading is to let a new hire keep tips they received directly for their own service.

### 3. Tip credit

New York allows a tip credit for food service workers only if "the total of tips received plus the wages" meets the total rate and the employee was notified under 146-2.2 ([12 NYCRR 146-1.3](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/12-NYCRR-146-1.3)).
An excluded new hire who keeps no tips must be topped up to the full minimum wage, so the credit is effectively unavailable during the waiting period.

### 4. Notice and records

Before employment the employer must give written notice of the pay rate, the tip credit, and "that extra pay is required if tips are insufficient", with a signed acknowledgment kept six years ([12 NYCRR 146-2.2](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/12-NYCRR-146-2.2)).
A pool employer must keep for six years a daily log of the tips each employee collected on each shift, the eligible occupations, "the shares of tips that each occupation is scheduled to receive", and what each employee received by date, and make them "regularly made available for participants" ([12 NYCRR 146-2.17](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/12-NYCRR-146-2.17)).
A waiting period changes the scheduled shares, so it belongs in those records and in the Employee's written notice.

Verdict: allowed with conditions.

## California

### 1. Exclusion

No employer or agent may "collect, take, or receive any gratuity or a part thereof that is paid, given to, or left for an employee by a patron" ([Cal. Lab. Code 351](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=LAB&sectionNum=351.)).
"Every gratuity is hereby declared to be the sole property of the employee or employees to whom it was paid, given, or left for" (same).
"Agent" means anyone other than the employer "having the authority to hire or discharge any employee or supervise, direct, or control the acts of employees" ([Cal. Lab. Code 350(d)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=LAB&sectionNum=350.)).
The leading case upheld employer-mandated pooling because, when "more than one employee ... directly serve the table of a patron, the gratuity is left for the 'employees'" and becomes "their sole property as against the employer, to be equitably distributed between them" ([Leighton v. Old Heidelberg, 219 Cal. App. 3d 1062 (1990)](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/3d/219/1062.html)).
The Labor Commissioner says mandatory pooling is allowed but "the policy must be fair and reasonable", and that pooled employees must be ones to whom the tip was paid, given or left for ([DLSE Tips and Gratuities FAQ](https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/FAQ_tipsandgratuities.html)).
California pool law rests on the theory that tips belong to the group of employees who served.
A new hire who served the customer, or worked the shift a jar tip was left for, is in that group.
A rule that pays them nothing from tips left partly for them is hard to square with "equitably distributed" and "fair and reasonable".
No California case or DLSE opinion on excluding a new hire or trainee was found.

### 2. Direct tips

Requiring the new hire to contribute their own tips to a pool that pays them nothing is the most exposed version, because the gratuity was left for them.
Card tips must be paid "not later than the next regular payday" after authorization ([Cal. Lab. Code 351](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=LAB&sectionNum=351.)).
In a counter-service Location with a shared jar and card tips that are not attributable to one person, the new hire's "own" tips cannot be separated out, which makes a clean "keep your own tips" carve-out impractical.

### 3. Tip credit

California has no tip credit, so this question does not arise ([DLSE FAQ](https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/FAQ_tipsandgratuities.html)).

### 4. Records

"Every employer shall keep accurate records of all gratuities received by him" ([Cal. Lab. Code 353](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=LAB&sectionNum=353.)).

### 5. What Opsell should do

Do not offer full exclusion in California.
A reduced, non-zero probation weight during the first days is less exposed, because the new hire still shares in tips left for the team, but whether a given weight is "fair and reasonable" is unsettled and should be the Organization's documented choice.
The 2025 amendment (SB 648, effective 2026-01-01) lets the Labor Commissioner cite employers for gratuities "taken or withheld" under 351(b), which raises enforcement exposure.

Verdict: unsettled, high risk.

## Massachusetts

### 1. Exclusion

"No employer or other person shall demand, request or accept from any wait staff employee, service employee, or service bartender any payment or deduction from a tip" ([M.G.L. c.149 s.152A(b)](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXXI/Chapter149/Section152A); text read via the [Wayback Machine copy](https://web.archive.org/web/2025/https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXXI/Chapter149/Section152A) because the live site was down).
The employer may not "retain or distribute in a manner inconsistent with this section any tip or service charge given directly to the employer" (same).
No one may require a wait staff employee to join a pool that distributes to anyone who is not wait staff, a service employee or a service bartender, and "An employer may administer a valid tip pool" ([s.152A(c)](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXXI/Chapter149/Section152A)).
A "tip" is money "given as an acknowledgment of any service performed by a wait staff employee", card tips included (s.152A(a)).
The Attorney General says tip sharing "is permitted, provided that the distribution of the tips is limited to wait staff employees, service employees and service bartenders", and the Act "eliminates any distinction between cash and credit card tips" ([AG Advisory 2004/3, amended January 2021](https://www.mass.gov/doc/attorney-generals-advisory-on-tips/download)).
A pool that moves a new hire's tips to other wait staff does not breach s.152A(c), because every recipient is eligible.
But the statute does not define a "valid tip pool", and s.152A(b) forbids any employer-required "deduction from a tip" except as the section allows.
Whether a pool that takes a wait staff employee's tips and pays them nothing is "valid" has no answer in the statute, the advisory, or any case found.
Employer-imposed service charges must go to staff "in proportion to the service provided by those employees" (s.152A(d)); that rule covers charges on the bill, not voluntary tips, but it shows the law's direction.

### 2-3. Direct tips and tip credit

Massachusetts allows a service rate (tip credit) only if the employer informs the employee in writing, "all tips received by the employee are either retained by him or her" or pooled under a pool that conforms with s.152A, and tips plus the service rate reach the minimum wage; otherwise the full minimum wage is due ([454 CMR 27.03(2)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/massachusetts/454-CMR-27-03)).
An excluded new hire keeps no tips, so the full minimum wage is due for the waiting period.

### 4. Remedies

Violations carry restitution "together with interest thereon at the rate of 12 per cent per annum", the penalties of section 27C, a private action under section 150, and no contracting out ([s.152A(f)-(g)](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXXI/Chapter149/Section152A)).
Tips remitted to the employer must be paid "by the end of the same business day" and never later than the s.148 wage deadline (s.152A(e)).

Verdict: unsettled, high risk; block full exclusion.

## Alaska

An employer "may not handle or take possession or control of an employee's tips" except as listed, including "redistributing tips to service employees under a tip pooling arrangement" ([8 AAC 15.907(a)-(b)](https://regulations.justia.com/states/alaska/title-8/part-1/chapter-15/article-6/section-8-aac-15-907), text current through 2024-11-28).
For that pool, "an employer shall provide written notice to all service employees of the tip pooling arrangement", any change "must be provided in writing on or before the payday before the payday on which the change becomes effective", and "a tip pooling arrangement may not be retroactive" (same).
A "tip pooling arrangement" is "an agreement under which a portion of an employee's tips is collected for distribution among service employees" (8 AAC 15.907(d)(2)), and service employees include servers, order-takers, cooks and dishwashers but not employees whose primary duty is managerial (d)(1).
The rule does not require that every service employee receive a share, so a waiting period spelled out in the written arrangement fits it.
Alaska bars a tip credit (prior note), so there is no credit interaction.
A 2021 public notice proposed changes on an "employer option for tip pooling"; whether they were adopted was not confirmed.

Verdict: allowed with conditions (written notice, advance written change, no retroactivity).

## Minnesota

Any gratuity "is the sole property of the employee", and "No employer may require an employee to contribute or share a gratuity received by the employee with the employer or other employees or to contribute any or all of the gratuity to a fund or pool operated for the benefit of the employer or employees" ([Minn. Stat. 177.24, subd. 3](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/177.24)).
Employees may share voluntarily, but "The agreement to share gratuities must be made by the employees without employer coercion or participation"; the employer may only safeguard and pay out shared tips on employees' request, report them for tax, and post the law (same).
"Pooling or sharing of gratuities may not be a condition of employment" ([Minn. R. 5200.0080, subp. 4](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/rules/5200.0080/)).
Dividing a gratuity among the direct service employees who served "in a given situation" is not a violation ([Minn. R. 5200.0080, subp. 8](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/rules/5200.0080/)), and DLI treats dividing a tip jar "among direct service employees working on the same shift" the same way ([DLI, Tips](https://www.dli.mn.gov/tips)).
Card tips must reach the employee in full by the next scheduled pay period ([subd. 3a](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/177.24)).
A waiting period is an employer-set pool term, which is "employer participation" in tip sharing.
The jar-division safe harbor covers dividing among the direct service employees on the shift, and a new hire serving that shift is one of them.

Verdict: clearly forbidden.

## Montana

"An employer may impose a tip pooling arrangement", and it may include servers, hosts, bussers, dishwashers, cooks or other employees ([MCA 39-3-218(1)-(2)](https://mca.legmt.gov/bills/mca/title_0390/chapter_0030/part_0020/section_0180/0390-0030-0020-0180.html)).
There is no minimum or maximum contribution percentage, but "employees may not be required to contribute to a tip pool more than the amount received in tips" (same, (2)).
The employer "shall notify its employees of any required tip pooling arrangement", may not participate, may not include exempt salaried supervisors or managers, must distribute by the regular payday, and must keep records "evidencing tips received and distributed pursuant to the tip pool" (same, (3)).
The statute (2021) replaced an older department policy that tip sharing had to be voluntary, as described in a [2008 department hearing decision](https://dli.mt.gov/hearings/decisions/2009/_docs/whdec354_2008.pdf) (not read first-hand).
Nothing in 39-3-218 requires every participant to receive a share.
Montana bars a tip credit for the employers covered by the prior note.

Verdict: allowed with conditions (notice, contribution cap, records).

## Nevada

It is unlawful to "Take all or part of any tips or gratuities bestowed upon the employees" or to credit tips toward the minimum wage, and nothing prevents employees "from entering into an agreement to divide such tips or gratuities among themselves" (NRS 608.160, as quoted in the [Labor Commissioner's Advisory Opinion AO-2025-08, 2025-08-12](https://labor.nv.gov/uploadedFiles/labornvgov/content/Employer/AO-2025-08%20Tip%20Pooling%20Policy.pdf)).
The Labor Commissioner says "Nevada case law permits mandatory tip pooling by employers", "Employers may include employees in different positions and ranks in mandatory tip pools", but "the employer may not keep any portion of the tips", citing Wynn Las Vegas v. Baldonado, 129 Nev. 734 (2013) (same).
The opinion also tells employers to follow the FLSA (same).
No Nevada rule on who must be included was found.

Verdict: allowed with conditions (federal rules).

## Oregon

"Employers ... may not include any amount received by employees as tips in determining the amount of the minimum wage" ([ORS 653.035(3)](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors653.html)).
BOLI tells workers "Your employer can require you to pool tips with other workers" and that "under federal regulations, management or the house cannot share in the pool" ([BOLI, Oregon Minimum Wage](https://www.oregon.gov/boli/workers/pages/minimum-wage.aspx)).
The full 2025 text of ORS chapters 652 and 653 was searched and contains no tip-pool or tip-pool notice provision; ORS 652.610 is about wage deductions and itemized statements.
No OAR 839-020 tip-pool notice rule could be located (see Gaps).
With no state rule on pool membership, the federal no-tip-credit rules govern.

Verdict: allowed with conditions (federal rules).

## Washington

"State law does not prohibit a tip pool amongst employees established by an employer", and "State law does not specify what employees may be included in a tip pool amongst employees, except that" employees exempt from the Minimum Wage Act, such as exempt managers, may not be included ([L&I ES.A.12, 2019-03-06, A.3-A.4](https://www.lni.wa.gov/workers-rights/_docs/esa12.pdf)).
"The department recommends that written policies be established to govern mandatory tip pooling arrangements, and that employees be informed of these policies" (same, A.3).
"Tips and gratuities due to an employee may not be used by the employer for any purpose except to administer a valid tip pool amongst qualifying employees" (same, A.1).
An employer "may not require an employee to contribute more than the employee actually received in tips to a mandatory tip pool", and card tips must be paid no later than wages for the same period (same, D).
Records must show the amount paid each pay period, tips included ([ES.A.12 C, citing RCW 49.46.070](https://www.lni.wa.gov/workers-rights/_docs/esa12.pdf); [L&I, Tips and Service Charges](https://www.lni.wa.gov/workers-rights/wages/tips-and-service-charges)).
RCW 49.46.160 governs automatic service charges, not tips, so it does not affect this question (ES.A.12 B).
Washington bars a tip credit.

Verdict: allowed with conditions (written policy, contribution cap, records).

## Trainee and new-hire case law

Searches for court decisions on excluding new hires, trainees or probationary employees from tip pools found none in any jurisdiction covered.
The closest authority is the treatment of employees excluded from a pool their tips went into: allowed in New York (Barenboim, assistant store managers) and federally (managers may be required to contribute, FOH 30d02(d)).
Trainee status does not change pool eligibility in any source read; eligibility turns on duties and occupation.
Training time spent not serving customers cannot carry a federal tip credit, because the credit is limited to hours in a tipped occupation ([29 CFR 531.59(b)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/29/531.59)).

## Constraints for Opsell

1. **The waiting-period setting is gated by the Location's state.**
   Allow it in Locations governed only by federal law and in TX, NY, AK, MT, NV, OR and WA.
   Block it in MN (pool terms set by the employer are forbidden), CA and MA (full exclusion is unsettled and high risk).
   Keep the gate list as data next to the existing tip credit and BOH gates, so a state can move without a code change.
2. **The setting is part of the effective-dated per-Location Tip Policy, not a per-Employee override.**
   It applies the same N days to every new Employee in a tip-eligible Position, which keeps it far from New York's "outer limit" on excluding classifications.
   Changes take effect only at a future date; in AK the effective date must be no earlier than the payday after the next payday, and no change may ever be backdated.
3. **Define "day worked" once and store it.**
   Count distinct Business Days on which the Employee clocked a shift in a tip-eligible Position at any Location of the Organization, and make the cutoff explicit (excluded on days 1 to N-1, eligible from day N).
   Show on each Tip Distribution which Employees were excluded for the waiting period and their day count.
4. **Exclusion withholds only pool distributions.**
   Declared direct cash tips always stay with the new hire, as today.
   Card tips and the counted jar may still go into the pool while the new hire is excluded in the allowed states, because federal law, NY (Barenboim) and the allowed states let tips go to other eligible Employees.
   The pool must still be split only among eligible Employees, so the waiting period can never route money to an ineligible person or leave money undistributed.
5. **Tip credit and the waiting period do not mix.**
   For an Employee in the waiting period, the payroll export must report zero tips retained from the pool and flag that no tip credit may be taken for those hours (federal 531.54(c)(2), NY 146-1.3, MA 454 CMR 27.03).
   Simplest rule: when a Location takes a tip credit, the waiting-period setting requires a confirmation that new hires in the waiting period are paid the full cash minimum wage.
6. **Never require a contribution larger than the Employee's own tips.**
   Opsell only pools the tips that were actually collected, so this holds by construction; keep it as an invariant test because MT and WA state it explicitly.
7. **Record the written notice.**
   Store the Tip Policy text shown to Employees, including the waiting period, with the version, effective date and each Employee's acknowledgment.
   This covers AK's written notice to all service employees, MT's notice of the required pool, the federal notice of required contributions, NY's six-year acknowledgment, and WA's and TX's written-policy recommendations.
8. **Keep per-shift records for excluded Employees too.**
   Record the tips each new hire collected on each shift (card and jar share) even while they draw nothing, and the scheduled shares including the waiting-period rule, for six years, available to participants (NY 146-2.17, Cal. Lab. Code 353, MT 39-3-218(3)(d)).
   The excluded Employee can see their own contributed tips and their waiting-period status.
9. **Offer a reduced probation weight as the softer alternative, not as the CA or MA workaround.**
   A non-zero Position weight during the first days keeps the new hire in the pool and is the safer design wherever exclusion is allowed.
   In CA and MA even a reduced weight should be the Organization's documented choice with counsel, because "fair and reasonable" (CA) and "valid tip pool" (MA) are undefined.
10. **MN Locations can run only employee-made sharing.**
    Opsell may record and pay out a sharing agreement that the Employees made themselves, but the Location may not configure a waiting period or any other pool term.
